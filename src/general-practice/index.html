Who this is really for

Be precise. This works best where AI use already exists and leaders want reassurance.

Primary fit

Where product market fit exists now.

  • GP Partners in England
  • Practice Managers in digitally active practices
  • PCN Clinical Directors
  • Practices already using AI scribes, admin copilots, or triage tools
  • Clinicians using AI independently for notes, admin, or learning
  • Teams being pressed to pause, justify, or formalise AI use

Secondary fit

Emerging, typically slower. Qualify hard to avoid drag.

  • Practices planning first formal AI adoption
  • PCNs coordinating shared AI tooling
  • ICB digital leads piloting AI with practices

Not a fit

Actively de qualify. These cycles are long and low yield.

  • Practices with zero AI use and no intention to pilot
  • Waiting for national guidance before doing anything
  • Looking for cheap AI tools
  • Asking “why do we need this if nothing has gone wrong yet”

Fast qualification and clean messaging

One question to open the door, plus what to stop leading with.

Qualification question

Ask this early:

“Are any clinicians in your practice already using AI, formally or informally, for notes, admin, or learning?”

Yes → strong fit No but planning to → possible fit No and not interested → disengage

What to lead with instead

Practical, local, defensible. No hype.

  • Local decisions and interim governance
  • Inspection ready evidence
  • Reduced cognitive load and time reclaimed
  • Clear boundaries for safe use

What to de emphasise

If you lead with these, you trigger scepticism and “NHS hype” instincts.

Productivity gains

Reframe as time reclaimed and reduced cognitive load.
