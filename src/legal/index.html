Where the exposure sits

Many firms have approved an enterprise AI tool and written a policy for using it. The harder part is making sure the two match day to day. Where they do not, three consequences may follow.

Client confidentiality

If a fee earner enters privileged material into an unapproved tool, the immediate concern is client confidentiality rather than technology alone. A breach is no less serious because a policy prohibited the relevant conduct.

Regulatory scrutiny

Firms must be able to demonstrate appropriate supervision of how work is produced, including work supported by AI. A firm may need to show that its controls operated in practice, not only that a policy was in place.

Professional indemnity

Professional indemnity insurers are beginning to take an interest in how firms govern AI use. Firms that can provide evidence of governance in practice may be better placed at renewal than those relying on a policy document alone.

Five questions your firm should be able to answer

  1. Which AI tools are approved for use with client data, and how is that enforced?
  2. How do you know that unapproved tools are not being used for client work?
  3. Who reviews AI assisted output before it leaves the firm, and is that review recorded?
  4. How are fee earners trained, and how often is that training refreshed?
  5. If something went wrong, could you reconstruct what was done, by whom and with what data?

If any of these answers currently relies on the policy alone, that is the place to begin.

From Policy to Practice

We help law firms make sure AI is used the way their policy intends. The work typically takes about six weeks, in three fixed fee phases.

Phase 1

Policy to practice review

Weeks 1 to 2
An anonymous, no blame picture of how the approved tool is really used, and a short report showing where practice departs from policy, with priorities agreed.

Phase 2

Strengthen policy and controls

Weeks 3 to 4
An AI policy rewritten as practical rules, a one page staff guide, an approved use list, a controls checklist for IT, and a briefing pack for your broker.

Phase 3

Training and assurance

Weeks 5 to 6
Role based training on the firm's own tool, a briefing for partners on their leadership responsibilities, and a light touch assurance plan the COLP can run.

You can commission all three phases or start with Phase 1. Each has a fixed fee, confirmed after an initial conversation.
